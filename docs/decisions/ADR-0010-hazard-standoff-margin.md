# ADR-0010: Hazard standoff margin

- **Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-17. Core behaviour covered by unit tests and an equivalence proof; Gazebo verification: see `docs/simulation/batch_findings.md`.
- **Context:** Reviewing the demo video, the user saw the robot driving "over the fire". The data agreed. In the first batch (no standoff, `docs/simulation/*_v1_no_standoff.md`), AegisNav runs that recorded positions (seeds 4–6) came within 0.60–0.66 m of a burning cell in every run, and the shortest-path baseline within 0.17–0.24 m. The robot detected the fires and replanned (logged discoveries and route cancellations), so this was not a detection failure. It was a planning-rule gap: the planner only *forbids* cells at risk ≥ `hard_risk_threshold` (0.92). A cell beside a flame (risk 0.4–0.6 from the model's halo) is merely costlier, so a route may brush past it. The robot is about 1 m long and a flame base about 0.8 m wide, so on screen it looks like driving into the fire. The previous summary metrics ("0/6 entered risk ≥ 0.92") were true and measured, but they did not capture this.
- **Decision:**
  1. New planner option `PlannerConfig.hazard_standoff_cells` (0–4, default 0). A move may not **enter** a cell within that many cells of a cell at or above the hard threshold, measured **through free space** (a wall stops the spread), in the forecast layer it arrives in. A move that starts inside the zone is allowed (so the robot can always leave a new fire next to it). With 0 the planner is unchanged; this was verified against the previous planner on 120 random queries (all `RoutePlan` fields identical).
  2. Both world presets use 2 cells = 1.0 m of free space beyond the burning cell, i.e. at least 1.5 m centre to centre. That is roughly a robot half-length (0.5 m) plus a flame radius (0.4 m) plus margin. It is a simulation rule, not a validated safety distance.
  3. The same value is passed to the triage ranking (`scenario_node`) so it does not choose goals the planner then refuses.
  4. The distance-only **baseline stays hazard-blind**: its planner config has no standoff and no hazard cost.
  5. `rescue_compound` forecast horizon 90 → 120 layers (240 s). With the margin, one leg on seed 1 lengthens (route total 175 m against 133 m) and its round trip no longer fit in 180 s, so the mission policy declined it (`forecast_horizon_insufficient`). The longer horizon fixes that without loosening any threshold.
  6. The mission recorder now reports `min_fire_distance_m` and `time_within_1m_of_fire_s` for every run, and `tools/analyze_batch.py` tabulates them, so this property is measured rather than inferred from exposure alone.
- **Consequences:**
  - Routes can be longer and a goal inside a standoff zone cannot be entered from outside; the mission policy then reports `target_route_unavailable` (triage sets the point aside). A room fully closed by the margin has no route.
  - The earlier batch is kept as `artifacts/batch_v1_no_standoff/` and is not comparable with the new one; the new batch re-runs both modes.
  - The margin applies to cells at or above the hard threshold in the *forecast*, so it moves with the spreading hazard. Cells outside it can still be passed at moderate risk at a cost; this is the intended trade-off, not a safety guarantee.
  - The mission-level claim changes from "never entered risk ≥ 0.92" to also stating the measured closest approach to fire.
